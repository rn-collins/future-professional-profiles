## Editorial and copy strategy

### Defensible thesis

**The advisor who sees beneath the listing.**

That position turns Mark’s most defensible combination—Kāneʻohe roots, finance, appraisal/analysis, brokerage leadership, transactions, and listening—into an audience promise. It avoids the interchangeable vocabulary of “dream home,” “top producer,” “luxury,” and “unparalleled service.”

### Recommended narrative hierarchy

1. Begin with the consequence: a property can be attractive and still be the wrong decision.
2. Establish the mechanism of judgment: finance and appraisal taught him to interrogate assumptions.
3. Establish place: Kāneʻohe and Oʻahu are lived systems, not a lifestyle backdrop.
4. Establish service: listening translates analysis into the client’s actual priorities.
5. Establish proof: leadership, audiences served, designations, and accurately attributed recognition.
6. End with the promise: make the reasoning clear enough that a client can choose—not merely comply.

### Voice rules

- Calm, specific, analytical, locally literate, and humane.
- Prefer questions, tradeoffs, and evidence over self-congratulation.
- Use `I would examine` in concept posts, not `I found`, unless Mark confirms a real case.
- Never invent dialogue, client outcomes, offer counts, motivations, ROI, testimonials, or private transaction mechanics.
- Avoid tourism language and performative localism; place knowledge should appear through decision detail.
- Separate public fact, Mark’s attributed belief, and RN’s proposed editorial strategy visually and semantically.

### High-value content franchises

1. **What the Listing Doesn’t Tell You** — document, condition, insurance, permit, basis, and downside education.
2. **Decision Before Transaction** — sell/buy/rent/renovate/wait sequencing.
3. **The Value Bridge** — show how a valuation moves from comparable sale to subject-specific conclusion.
4. **Oʻahu Without the Theater** — segment monthly data by decision, property type, price band, and place.
5. **Terms Are Money Too** — negotiation preparation and non-price leverage, using composites or approved cases.
6. **The Building Is the Asset** — condo governance, reserves, insurance, assessments, financing, and expert boundaries.
7. **Place Changes the Model** — neighborhood/property-form intelligence without ranking communities or stereotyping residents.
8. **The Referral Trigger** — concise situations where Mark’s appraisal/analysis background is unusually useful.
