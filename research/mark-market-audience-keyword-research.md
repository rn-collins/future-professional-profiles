## Direct answer
Public evidence supports an appraisal-informed property-decision position. It does not support describing this as exhaustive quantitative keyword research. The defensible whitespace is a consistent editorial system that shows assumptions, comparable evidence, downside, building-level finances, and what would change the recommendation.

## Material audiences
Local/first-time buyers; move-up/high-value clients; condominium buyers and sellers; investors/nonresident owners; military/VA-eligible buyers pending experience confirmation; professional referral partners.

## Priority search-intent clusters
Oʻahu condominium diligence; value and seller pricing; Oʻahu market interpretation; neighborhood/property-type comparison; flood and insurance exposure; investor underwriting; VA/military decisions.

No defensible volume, CPC, or keyword-difficulty measures were available from free public search. These clusters are based on current search-result repetition, authoritative consumer issues, and local market/regulatory evidence.

## Competitor finding
Locations, HiEstates, List Sotheby’s, Caron B Realty, Hawaiʻi Life, and Compass/Yvonne Ahearn collectively cover market reporting, relocation video, luxury curation, lifestyle, community reporting, and general valuation/investment subjects. Representative review did not reveal a recurring evidence-linked, appraisal-style decision-memo system. This is qualitative whitespace, not proof of exclusive ownership or measured share of search.

## Boundaries
Confirm “former appraiser” or “appraisal-trained” with Mark. Do not imply current appraisal licensure. Route legal, tax, lending, insurance, engineering, and appraisal questions appropriately. Follow Hawaiʻi advertising, leasehold-disclosure, and fair-housing requirements. Substantiate awards and reconcile directory information.
