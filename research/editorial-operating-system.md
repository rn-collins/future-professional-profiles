# Editorial operating system

Updated: 3 September 2026  
Status: implementation specification for the Mark H. Young and Samuel “Sam” Wolff professional-presence prototypes

## Purpose

This system turns verified public evidence into useful editorial assignments without pretending that a subject commissioned, approved, wrote, published, or achieved the proposed work. It is designed to improve the quality of human editorial decisions—not to automate a person’s identity.

## Publication states

Every item must occupy exactly one state:

1. **Evidence** — an accessible source or retained archival record.
2. **Verified fact** — a narrowly written claim directly supported by evidence.
3. **Attributed statement** — a view or statement assigned to a person only where the record supports that attribution.
4. **Strategic interpretation** — RN Studio’s explanation of why the record may matter to an audience.
5. **Proposed concept** — an uncommissioned future editorial possibility.
6. **Draft for review** — copy built from approved evidence but not approved for publication.
7. **Approved publication** — a subject or authorized publisher has approved the exact copy and media.
8. **Measured publication** — a live item with real, dated performance data.

No interface, export, or workflow may silently promote an item from one state to another.

## Commissioning sequence

1. Select one audience and one real decision that audience faces.
2. Select a content-series promise appropriate to that decision.
3. Attach the minimum evidence necessary to support the intended factual claims.
4. Separate source fact, attributed view, analysis, hypothetical example, and proposed recommendation.
5. Draft against the subject-specific voice rules stored in `app/data.ts`.
6. Conduct claim-level fact, attribution, privacy, rights, and reputational review.
7. Obtain subject approval before publishing in first person or through a subject-controlled channel.
8. Publish with source links, dates, accessible media, and a corrections path.
9. Measure actual audience behavior; never backfill or simulate engagement.
10. Feed substantive questions, corrections, and qualified conversations into the next editorial cycle.

## Mark H. Young editorial test

A Mark item is ready for review only if it answers all five questions:

- What consequential property decision is being clarified?
- What does the listing, photograph, or generic statistic fail to explain?
- What appraisal, market, or process evidence supports the explanation?
- How does the analysis protect a client rather than merely advertise an agent?
- Is the call to action a useful next decision rather than manufactured urgency?

His editorial signature is disciplined judgment made visible. The voice should be measured, locally fluent, specific, and protective. It should avoid luxury theater, unqualified rankings, generic inspiration, invented client emotion, and transaction claims that cannot be disclosed.

## Sam Wolff editorial test

A Sam item is ready for review only if it answers all six questions:

- What public, physical, financial, or regulatory system is being examined?
- What primary record or authoritative source anchors the piece?
- What is documented fact, what is Sam’s directly attributed position, and what is editorial analysis?
- Who experiences the cost, delay, risk, loss of access, or implementation burden?
- Which credible countervailing interest or uncertainty remains?
- Does a technical conclusion require a credentialed expert rather than Sam’s inferred authority?

His editorial signature is systems translation with honest tradeoffs. The voice should be curious, plainspoken, civically serious, and exact about attribution. It must not speak for a board, imply scientific credentials, overstate a current-role index, or turn Hawaiʻi into generic investment scenery.

## Ninety-day decision model

The purpose of the first 90 days is not maximum volume. It is to establish a recognizable method, demonstrate its range, and convert the questions it earns into durable public resources.

### Phase 1: establish the method

- Publish the clearest articulation of the subject’s decision philosophy.
- Launch one signature series.
- Demonstrate one sourced explanation in more than one format.
- Record substantive questions and evidence behavior.

### Phase 2: demonstrate range

- Apply the method to multiple audience decisions without diluting the position.
- Add one deeper case, system, or decision anatomy.
- Introduce a recurring primary-source or market interpretation.
- Review attribution, rights, and audience fit before increasing volume.

### Phase 3: convert earned attention

- Turn recurring questions into searchable evergreen resources.
- Publish one high-utility downloadable or diagnostic artifact.
- Invite one narrowly scoped conversation appropriate to the evidence and role.
- Attribute inquiries to their originating question, series, and audience.

## Measurement hierarchy

The hierarchy is intentionally ordered from evidence of understanding to business or civic consequence:

1. Evidence-source opens and full-brief completion
2. Saves, substantive replies, and specific follow-up questions
3. Returning readers and newsletter subscriptions
4. Referral introductions and qualified conversations
5. Content-assisted client, advisory, collaboration, or stakeholder outcomes

Reach, impressions, and reactions are diagnostic context—not proof of trust, expertise, or commercial value.

## Human authority gates

The following cannot be solved through better software alone:

- approval to publish in the subject’s voice;
- confirmation of private or currently indexed-only employment facts;
- client consent and confidentiality review;
- family-image and third-party-image permission;
- authorization to connect a publishing, CRM, email, or analytics account;
- acceptance of reputational and legal risk for a final public statement.

The system should surface these gates at the moment they matter and retain the decision; it should never infer consent from public availability.
