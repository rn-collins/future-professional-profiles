## Executive decision

The isolated experience should not sell “social-media management.” It should demonstrate a professional-authority system built around one unusually credible but publicly under-expressed advantage: Mark’s company biography says he applied a finance education in commercial and residential appraisal/analysis before becoming a REALTOR®. His HBR directory identity, company leadership, local roots, selected independent directory entries, and long first-party recognition record make that foundation believable. What the public record does **not** yet show is a durable body of work that makes his reasoning inspectable.

The recommended position is:

> **The appraisal-trained advisor who makes the reasoning visible before a consequential property decision.**

This is stronger than “top agent,” “local expert,” or “trusted advisor.” Those phrases are generic and require proof that the current public surface cannot consistently supply. The proposed position is rooted in documented history and can be demonstrated through tools, annotated evidence, decision cases, and disciplined publishing.

## Audience decisions, in priority order

### 1. Households coordinating a move

Their problem is not “find listings.” It is sequence and exposure: buy first, sell first, carry both, accept a contingency, or change the move. The best artifact is a transparent scenario tool that labels every assumption and directs financing questions to a qualified lender.

### 2. Residential investors and sophisticated buyers

Their question is whether the thesis survives contact with the documents, physical condition, permits, recurring costs, basis, and exit buyer. This is the purest expression of the appraisal-trained position, provided the site distinguishes broker analysis from a formal appraisal.

### 3. Condo buyers and owners

The whitespace is not another FAQ. It is a document reading room that explains what each record can establish, what it cannot, and which question belongs to legal, insurance, reserve, inspection, engineering, or lending expertise.

### 4. Sellers in transition

The content must reveal preparation, segment, likely buyer, sequence, and downside logic—not promise price or timing.

### 5. Professional referrers

Attorneys, lenders, CPAs, wealth advisors, and past clients need fit signals: which decisions make Mark a strong introduction, how he works, and where his role ends. A referral memo can convert authority into qualified introductions without performative posting.

## Competitive and search conclusion

The visible category is crowded with listings, neighborhood lifestyle, luxury imagery, credential-led bios, and monthly market recaps. HBR owns the canonical market data; mature teams already translate it into readable reports. Mark should cite the primary source rather than imitate it.

The most defensible whitespace is **decision-specific interpretation**:

- What could reverse the first impression?
- What does this document prove, and what remains unknown?
- Which market number changes this decision, for this property and objective?
- Which assumption breaks the investment thesis?
- How should two connected transactions be sequenced?

No paid keyword database, impression data, or reliable local search-volume dataset was available. Search recommendations are directional, based on live result patterns and recurring audience questions. They must not be displayed as quantitative demand.

## Best first pilot

**Five Facts Before the Feeling** is the recommended lead pilot. Each edition starts with the emotional first impression of a property and reveals five less glamorous facts that could change the decision. The system can become:

- a concise field video;
- an evidence-linked LinkedIn document;
- a durable owned search page;
- a buyer worksheet;
- a referral proof artifact; and
- a source packet for follow-up conversations.

Publication gates:

1. obtain imagery/document permission;
2. name and date each source;
3. distinguish broker reasoning from appraisal, legal, inspection, engineering, insurance, reserve, tax, and lending advice;
4. avoid private client inference;
5. have Mark approve voice and conclusion;
6. preserve a correction/withdrawal route.
