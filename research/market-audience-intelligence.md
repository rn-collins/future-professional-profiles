## Direct answer

The strongest defensible position is not “another Hawaiʻi real-estate personality.” The public search surface is already crowded with listing tours, generalized market recaps, neighborhood guides, luxury superlatives, and contact funnels. The usable whitespace is **decision intelligence whose evidence remains visible**:

- **Mark:** appraisal-trained counsel that shows how a buyer, seller, or investor should reason beneath a property’s presentation.
- **Sam:** plain-language translation between property decisions and the public systems—energy, infrastructure, permitting, resilience, and community process—that shape feasibility and operating cost.

This distinction is an editorial strategy derived from documented experience. It is not a claim either subject has adopted it, approved the prototype, or produced the proposed content.

## Market conditions that create audience need

### Residential property decisions

- The Honolulu Board of REALTORS® reported 2025 Oʻahu resale medians of **$1,139,000 for single-family homes** and **$507,250 for condos**, with 2,890 and 4,408 sales respectively. That price level makes pre-offer reasoning, downside analysis, and sequencing consequential rather than ornamental. [HBR 2025 annual report](https://www.hicentral.com/mpr/mpr-2025-12.php)
- By July 2026 HBR’s public market surface showed a **$1,224,500 single-family median** and **$504,500 condo median**. Month-level figures are volatile and should be dated, segmented, and never converted into blanket forecasts. [HBR market reports](https://www.hicentral.com/market-press-releases.php)
- Census QuickFacts reports a **$897,500 median owner-occupied housing value**, **$3,111 median monthly owner cost with a mortgage**, **$2,083 median gross rent**, and **59.8% owner-occupied rate** for Honolulu County in 2020–2024. These are population estimates, not transaction statistics, but they establish the scale of household housing decisions. [U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/honolulucountyhawaii/HEA775224)
- Updated Oʻahu flood maps became effective in June 2026; the City directs owners to official flood, sea-level-rise, shoreline-change, heat, and tree-canopy tools. Risk-map interpretation therefore has immediate insurance, permit, renovation, and diligence relevance. [City DPP resources](https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/resources/); [Resilient Oʻahu FIRM update](https://www.resilientoahu.org/firm-update)

### Commercial, operating-cost, and public-system decisions

- Oʻahu office vacancy fell to **12.40% in Q1 2026**, while Class A vacancy fell to **8.37%**; Colliers also flagged fuel and energy costs as an operating-expense and tenant-demand pressure. [Colliers office report](https://www.colliers.com/en/research/hawaii/oahu-office-market-report-q1-2026)
- Oʻahu industrial vacancy remained historically tight at **1.40% in Q1 2026**, with elevated pricing tied to supply constraints and replacement cost. [Colliers industrial report](https://www.colliers.com/en/research/hawaii/oahu-industrial-market-report-q1-2026)
- Oʻahu retail vacancy reached **6.01% in Q1 2026** after major anchor closures returned large-format space to the market. [Colliers retail report](https://www.colliers.com/en/research/hawaii/oahu-retail-market-report-q1-2026)
- Honolulu sewer charges began annual increases in 2026 and apply to residential and commercial users, liquid-waste haulers, and new development connections. This is a concrete example of a public-system decision entering private operating models. [Honolulu ENV fee schedule](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/sewer-fee-rates/)
- Hawaiʻi’s 2026 alternative-fuels study describes the state as a leader in distributed energy resources, while the City’s adopted/planning materials continue to connect development, infrastructure, resilience, and climate risk. [Hawaiʻi State Energy Office study](https://energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/HSEO-Alternative-Fuels-Study-Revised-May-2026.pdf); [Oʻahu General Plan review](https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/wp-content/uploads/sites/56/2025/12/1-GP-Review-Report.pdf)

## Qualitative search-demand findings

The crawl tested exact-name, role, market, and natural-language problem queries. It did **not** obtain impressions, clicks, keyword volume, or advertising competition. Repeated result families establish discoverable question supply—not the number of people asking.

1. Oʻahu buyer queries repeatedly returned fee-simple/leasehold explanations, condo/HOA reserves and assessments, insurance/flood exposure, permits, short-term-rental rules, financing, and resale value.
2. Current market queries returned HBR reports, brokerage recaps, tables, and videos. The result set is strong on “what changed” and weaker on “which decision does this change—and what evidence would reverse the answer?”
3. CRE transition queries returned national diligence lists and institutional primers: cap rate, NOI, leases, tenant risk, vacancy, capex, operating statements, environmental/zoning review, financing and exit assumptions.
4. Honolulu owner/operator queries surfaced new flood maps, building-permit workflows, sewer-fee increases, land-use rules, resilience tools, energy cost and grid-transition records.
5. Hawaiʻi Kai/community queries surfaced zoning, park access/safety, watershed, wildlife, infrastructure and development disputes. These require primary-record attribution rather than generic thought leadership.
6. Exact-name results are asymmetric: Mark has a coherent first-party professional and transaction footprint but no confidently resolved LinkedIn profile; Sam has an indexed LinkedIn identity and civic record but an unverified present commercial role and no independently established deal record.

## Recommended editorial portfolio

### Mark — “clear counsel before commitment”

1. **The Pre-Offer Evidence Brief:** one property; five unglamorous facts; source, consequence, unresolved question, and specialist referral.
2. **Sell First, Buy First, or Build a Bridge?:** interactive sequencing scenarios with no universal answer.
3. **The Condo Document Reading Room:** reserves, insurance, assessments, minutes, maintenance, and what each can/cannot establish.
4. **One Number, Three Markets:** take an HBR headline and show why price band, property type, neighborhood, and objective change interpretation.
5. **Appraisal Habits for Non-Appraisers:** educational reasoning without representing that a broker analysis is a formal appraisal.

### Sam — “put the place back into the model”

1. **Public Meeting, Private Consequence:** annotate one official record and map consequences for resident, owner, operator and implementer.
2. **Change the Asset, Change the Questions:** a residential assumption beside its commercial replacement; launch only after current role verification.
3. **The Oʻahu Operating-Cost Map:** dated changes in sewer, energy, insurance/flood, permits and infrastructure with primary links.
4. **Follow the Pathway:** trace water, electricity, waste, access or a permit from public system to property feasibility.
5. **Tradeoff Ledger:** fact, stakeholder need, implementation constraint, uncertainty, and question still unanswered.

## Measurement without vanity metrics

Track evidence opens, source-to-post click-through, completed decision tools, saved scenarios, qualified questions, referral introductions, consultation requests, corrections, and content-assisted conversations. Never fabricate historical engagement or promise outcomes. Establish a baseline only after launch, preserve UTM/referrer definitions, and show low-volume data honestly.

## Material limitations and disconfirming evidence

- No measured keyword volume, search-console data, customer interviews, CRM records, or conversion history was available. Audience needs are evidence-backed hypotheses, not validated demand forecasts.
- Mark’s company biography and recognition ledger are first-party claims; public-platform production figures are volatile and methodology-dependent.
- Sam’s current employer, responsibilities, commercial deal history, license status, client outcomes and investment results were not independently established. His CRE content lanes must remain conditional.
- A board’s action cannot be assigned to Sam without individual vote, authorship, or quotation evidence.
- Publicly accessible photographs are not automatically licensed for republication.
- Search engines omit authenticated, deleted, unindexed and paywalled material. Diminishing returns were reached when new queries repeated the same result families and no new identity-resolved consequential evidence appeared.

## Recommendations requiring subject input

Before a real campaign, interview each subject and at least three representatives of each priority audience. Ask for current role confirmation, controlled work examples, objections heard repeatedly, existing conversion data, approved images, claims they will personally stand behind, and content boundaries. The prototype should continue displaying strategy as RN’s proposal until that verification occurs.
